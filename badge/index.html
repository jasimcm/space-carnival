Under ₹50. Inside 40 × 40 mm.
Soldered by a first-timer in 15 minutes.

Design the official PCB badge for the next TinkerSpace Carnival: the circuit, the board shape and the artwork. The winning design gets fabricated and handed to every attendee as a keepsake they solder themselves.

It must cost under ₹50 to build, PCB and battery included, and it should say something about our local maker culture.

Who can join: anyone. Students, makers, first-timers. No PCB experience needed.

What you send: a public GitHub repo with your schematic, Gerbers, a BOM with prices and a render or photo, submitted as a link on the TinkerHub app.

RULE 01

Budget

Total BOM under ₹50 per badge, including the PCB and battery.

RULE 02

Board size

Must fit within 40 × 40 mm. Any shape inside that.

RULE 03

Power

A 3 V coin cell, or USB.

RULE 04

Assembly

Must give attendees a real soldering experience. Through-hole parts recommended.

RULE 05

Theme

Represents local maker culture: Kerala, Kochi, our communities, language and stories.

RULE 06

Manufacturer

Designed for Lion Circuits. Check their capabilities before you finalise.

Show your costing in the BOM: one row per part, with quantity, unit price at 300 units and a source link. Count the PCB at its quoted price for your board size.

Why a badge?

for many attendees, it's the first time holding a soldering iron.

We want them to walk away having built something that works and that they are proud to wear. Every attendee assembles their own badge at the Carnival, so it has to be solderable by a beginner in about 15–20 minutes.

The badge is a small piece of art that says where it came from. Opening the design up lets more people learn PCB design, and the winner sees their board made in the hundreds. At hundreds of units every rupee matters, hence ₹50.

make it useful, not just pretty.

A badge doesn't have to only blink. A USB-powered badge saves the battery cost and can be something people keep using after the Carnival. Microcontrollers with built-in USB (like the CH552) keep the cost low.

    Questions?

    Can I use a microcontroller?

    Yes, as long as the total badge stays under ₹50. If it needs programming, tell us how it gets flashed for hundreds of units.

    Can I make a purely analog badge?

    Yes. LEDs, 555 timers and transistor circuits are all welcome.

    Battery or USB?

    Either. A 3 V coin cell, a 90 mAh Li-Po or USB power are all fine; just include the cost in your BOM. A USB badge skips the battery cost and can do something useful when plugged into a computer.

    Does the ₹50 include the PCB?

    Yes. ₹50 is the total for everything on the badge, including the PCB and the battery.

    Can I use SMD parts?

    Yes, but attendees must still get to solder something. Through-hole parts are easier for beginners.

    I've never designed a PCB. Can I still join?

    Yes. Start with EasyEDA (see Never made a PCB?) and come to the Ask Anything call.

    Can I work in a team?

    Team size is still being decided. We'll update this page soon.
